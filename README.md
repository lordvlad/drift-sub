# Drift

A quiet underwater exploration toy: steer a small yellow submarine through an endless, procedurally generated sea (three.js).

- **Desktop:** `W`/`S` thrust, `A`/`D` turn, mouse to steer (click to capture), `Space` rise, `C` dive, `Shift` burst; with the manoeuvring kit `Q`/`E` strafe left/right.
- **Waves:** sea state follows your local wind (Open-Meteo, location rounded to ~11 km, never stored); falls back to a slow random drift if location is denied or offline. The "Waves" button cycles auto → still → calm → windy → gusty → stormy.
- **Controls UI:** every control is an icon button (hover for the label); tapping one briefly shows its new state (e.g. "lamps on") in the same quiet line as the calming notes.
- **Light:** time of day follows your clock (dawn, morning, day, evening, night hues; "Time" button overrides). The sub starts **without** lamps: buy the headlights in the shop (per sub), then `F` or the "Lamps" button cycles auto (on when dark or deep) / on / off. Until then the lamp buttons and `F` are hidden / inactive.
- **Fullscreen:** a dismissible banner suggests it a few seconds after diving (hidden where the Fullscreen API is missing, e.g. iPhone Safari).
- **PWA:** installable (manifest + service worker; works offline after the first visit). Add `?nosw` to skip the service worker.
- **Viewfinder:** the camera button (or `P`) enters viewfinder mode: first-person view from the sub's nose, you can turn/aim (mouse, `A`/`D`, drag) but not accelerate or strafe. `Space`/`Enter`/shutter takes the shot, the Lamps button (or `F`, if you own headlights) works in it, `Esc`/Exit leaves. With a zoom objective the magnifier button, mouse wheel or `+`/`−` step through 1× / 2× / 4× (FOV 58° narrows to 31° / 16°; the snapshot and its scoring use the zoomed FOV, so a distant animal in a 4× shot counts like a close one; zoom resets when you leave).
- **Shop:** the cart button opens a full-screen shop with your pearl balance (◎), upgrades for the active sub and the four submarines. Buying shows a toast; buttons are disabled when you can't afford the item or lack its prerequisite. Upgrades are bought **per submarine** and never transfer. Items: headlights ◎400; zoom objective 2× ◎1,200; zoom objective 4× ◎4,000 (needs 2×); hull repair kit ◎150 (consumable, repairs the active sub fully at once; disabled while undamaged); propeller Mk II ◎2,000 (+20% thrust), Mk III ◎6,000 (+40% total, needs Mk II); hull strengthener I ◎1,500 (+10 m max dive depth), II ◎5,000 (+20 m total, needs I); manoeuvring kit ◎3,500 (sideways thrusters with the same acceleration and inertia as the main motor; on touch the left lever becomes a 2-D joystick: x = strafe, y = thrust, never turning). Snapshot prices stay hidden until sold; there is no sell-all.
- **Submarines:** Lemon (free, 60 m, the all-rounder), Manta (◎45,000, 45 m, damage ×1.5, thrust ×1.05, turning ×1.6: very agile), Barracuda (◎80,000, 45 m, damage ×1.7, thrust ×1.7: fast), Squid (◎150,000, 95 m: deepest, damage ×2.4, thrust ×0.9: most fragile). Each sub keeps its own upgrades and its own hull damage. Once you own a second sub a swap button appears in the top bar: it cycles to the next owned sub (or use "Switch" in the shop), swapping the model in place and keeping position, heading and speed. You can't switch while deeper than the other sub could survive. Fleet state (`drift-fleet`) and pearls persist on this device; `src/fleet.js` holds the stats and item catalogue.
- **Equipment & prices (`src/features/equipment.js`, catalogue in `src/fleet.js`):** every item price is multiplied per submarine (Lemon ×1, Manta ×1.5, Barracuda ×2, Squid ×3, consumables too); the repair kit costs `150 × (0.3 + 1.7 × damage)` × that factor (cheap scratches, dear wrecks) and the shop shows the current price. Extra items: quiet propeller Mk I / II (animals notice you from 20% / 40% less far, softer prop sound; `stealth.noise` in `alarm()`), red-light filter (4th lamp mode "red": red beam; white lamps mildly startle shy animals ahead of the sub within 15 m at night, red does not), hydrophone (`H`: compass arc below the top bar with bearings and distances of whales / orcas / dolphins / giant squid / big plug-in animals within 250 m, soft panned pings), sonar ping (`O`, 12 s recharge: expanding ring plus a round radar of animals and landmarks within 120 m for ~6 s), wide-angle lens (0.6× viewfinder step, ~86° FOV), strobe light (`R` in the viewfinder: the shutter fires a flash that lights a dark shot, 2 s recharge, `shot.strobe`), album expansions I / II / III (+50 / +100 / +200 slots on top of 100: bought on any sub but shared by the whole album, `fleet.shared`, `setMaxShots()`).
- **Selling:** every snapshot is priced when taken (pearls ◎), mostly for skill (`src/photo.js`): how much of the frame the animal fills (a steep curve: a shark at 25 m is worth about ⅓ of the same shark filling a third of the frame; a distant speck is nearly worthless; zoom only counts through the fill), composition (centre or rule-of-thirds points score, the frame edge does not), sharpness (motion blur, see below), light (measured on the captured image: lamps/strobe at depth or night, silhouettes in the dark pay less), then rarity (species value), group size (a school or pod also counts by the area it covers), species variety, and scenery as a backdrop (coral, light rays, visible landmarks multiply a well-framed animal; an empty seascape is worth only a couple of pearls), plus golden-hour and deep-dive bonuses. Prices are hidden: you only learn what a shot was worth after selling it, so choose what to sell by judging the composition yourself. Compositions you already sold are remembered (up to 400, on this device): each earlier sale of a similar shot (same species, group size, side of frame, scenery, depth band and light) cuts the price by ×0.55, down to ×0.1. Pearls are kept on this device.
- **Motion blur:** a shot taken while the sub is moving fast or turning fast is smeared (directional + radial blur baked into the snapshot at capture time) and sells for much less (down to ×0.15); slow drifting (< ~1 m/s, gentle turns) stays sharp. Zoom magnifies every shake (4× with a gentle 0.1 rad/s turn is already ×0.6), wide-angle forgives. The viewfinder crosshair ring trembles and widens when a shot would come out blurred. Every snapshot record carries a small JSON descriptor `rec.shot` (spots with fill/centredness, biome, landmarks in view, depth, blur, lit/strobe, ...) for commissions and bestiary stars.
- **Shy animals:** how far animals keep from the sub grows steeply with its speed. Drift in slowly for close-ups; rush in and fish schools scatter (individual fish dart away), sharks drop their curiosity and bolt, dolphins/orcas/mantas/giant squid turn and speed off, whales can't outrun you but lumber away. Swim speed is flexible: each animal cruises, sprints when scared (sharks ~11 m/s, dolphins ~11, orcas ~12, giant squid jets ~8, mantas ~6, whales only ~1.5× cruise) with real acceleration, and tires after a long sprint.
- **Time of day:** at night about half the fish schools are hidden in the reef, firefly squid are much more common, sharks and giant squid show up more, dolphins and mantas less; dusk and dawn sit in between.
- **Species & depths (m):** fish — lyretail anthias 2–28, yellow tang 2–30, blue chromis 3–40, blue-green chromis 3–32, moon wrasse 4–38, emperor red snapper 15–55, royal gramma 8–48 (rare), sardine 3–50, golden trevally 6–45, blue mackerel 8–60, yellowtail fusilier 5–40, glassy sweeper 28–60 (rare), rainbow runner 5–55 (rare); sharks — blacktip 3–35, grey reef 5–45, tiger 12–60 (each with its own markings); whales — humpback 8–50, blue 15–60; squid — bigfin reef squid 2–30 (schools of 5–18), firefly squid 25–60 (glowing schools of 30–90, rare), giant squid 40–60 (solitary, very rare); manta ray 3–40; dolphins 1–25 (pods of 3–9); orcas 2–40 (pods of 2–6). Every large animal stays clear of the seabed. Definitions live in `src/species.js`.
- **Open-ocean and cave / wreck animals (`src/groups/pelagic.js`, models in `src/models/pelagic.js`, fact sheets in `src/data/pelagic-sheets.js`):** ten species in one fauna group. The barracuda's places (open water): yellowfin tuna 6–55 (fast schools of 10–34), mahi-mahi 2–24 (groups of 3–10 near the surface), great barracuda 3–36 (loose, nearly motionless groups that burst away), sailfish 3–40 (rare, a slow cruiser that now and then sprints at a school), scalloped hammerhead 10–58 (rare schools of 6–22, mostly at dusk and dawn, around seamounts and drop-offs). The manta's places (caves, arches, wrecks, reef mazes): green moray 4–45 (looks out of its own den rock, comes out to hunt at night), Atlantic goliath grouper 8–55 (rare, hangs by wrecks and caves, turns to face a slow sub), hawksbill turtle 3–35 (rare, now and then rises to the shallow edge of its band for air), common octopus 3–50 (camouflaged crawler: flushes red, inks and jets away when startled, only photographable up close), pennant coralfish 3–40 (small schools in reef passages). Spawning follows `field.biomeAt` and the landmark list; the active sub multiplies spawn rates (barracuda ×2.3 open water, manta ×2.3 caves, squid and lemon in between) and unlocks a second school/den of each, but every species can occur for every sub where its biome is.
- **Dark sea, trench and vent animals (`src/groups/deep.js`, models in `src/models/deep.js`, fact sheets in `src/data/deep-sheets.js`):** twelve species for the squid sub's places in one fauna group. Pelagic: lanternfish 20–110 (glowing schools of 22–76 that rise toward the surface at night), crown jelly 35–120 (blooms of 1–6 pulsing jellies carried by a slow current, glowing blue ring), humpback anglerfish 45–120 (glowing lure), gulper eel 55–120 (rare, glowing tail tip), vampire squid 50–120 (rare, glowing arm tips, fins flapped like wings), oarfish 30–110 (rare, 5–8 m), sperm whale 30–120 (rare, 11–15 m; solid body, other animals keep away). Seabed: dumbo octopus 40–120 (hovers over the bottom, flapping its ear fins), giant isopod 45–120 (a leg wave runs along its body as it crawls). Vents (clustered at the real chimneys / magma rifts from `field.featuresNear`): yeti crab 25–120 (clumps waving hairy claws), vent shrimp 15–120 (dense swarms in the plume), vent octopus 30–120 (rare, stays within ~14 m of its vent). Spawning is weighted by `field.biomeAt` (deep, trench, vent, magma) and the active sub: the squid sub meets them at full rate, the other subs mostly only their shallow tail (~20–45% as often). Everything is shy (fear grows with the sub's speed), has flexible speed (cruise, burst, stamina), keeps its depth band and never enters the seabed. Bioluminescence is vertex glow (visible with the lamps off) plus soft halo sprites on lures; test hook: `window.__game.deep.pose('angler', { dist: 3 })` freezes a species in front of the sub.
- **Damage:** hidden, no health bar. Hard bumps hurt: rocks and steep ground most, coral and whales a lot, flat sand only a little; ploughing through seaweed scrapes the hull. The sub's fragility multiplies the damage. Damage shows as a red glow growing from the screen edges, a lower safe dive depth (down to 40% of the sub's maximum at worst), and cracks on the camera lens (visible in the viewfinder and baked into snapshots, which sell for much less). The sub repairs itself slowly (a full wreck mends in about 12 minutes of play); a repair kit fixes it at once.
- **Bestiary:** the book button opens 24 numbered pages, one per species. A page unlocks once you take a snapshot in which the animal is recognisable (at least ~3% of the frame); until then it shows only its number and a depth hint. Unlocked pages show your own snapshot (cropped around the animal; a later, closer shot replaces it), a photo from Wikipedia/Wikimedia Commons (loaded on demand), a short description, a fun fact and links to Wikipedia, iNaturalist and (for fish) FishBase. Progress is kept on this device.
- **Bestiary stars & research grants:** every page holds 1–3 stars (rules in `src/stars.js`): ★ seen, ★★ a good portrait (animal ≥ ~30% of the frame height, or a school/pod that fills it; centred, sharp, lens not badly cracked), ★★★ a special moment per species/class (mother and calf, an orca/dolphin pod, glowing animals in the dark with lamps off, predator and prey together, a rare animal in its signature landmark, a blue whale or giant squid at all …; unknown species get ★★★ for a good portrait of a rare animal at night or near a landmark). Grid cards show the stars; the page shows the next star's hint, deliberately vague. The page picture is the one with the most stars, then the largest animal. A page unlock and each new star pay a one-time research grant (★ = species value ×1.5, min ◎40; ★★ ×3; ★★★ ×6; skipped levels are paid too), announced in the toast ("New page: Grey reef shark +◎135") and never paid twice. Saves from before stars migrate to ★ without retroactive grants.
- **Commissions:** the clipboard button opens three rotating photo requests (aquarium / magazine / research station) generated from your depth reach, discovered pages and the landmarks that exist. A snapshot that satisfies one is flagged (the save toast says "fulfils a commission", never a price); selling that very snapshot adds the bonus (≈1.5–3× a typical such shot) to the sale ("Sold for ◎ 65 (commission bonus ◎ 55)"). Done or expired (~36 h) requests are replaced; "Not now" swaps one out; state in `localStorage['drift-commissions']`; code in `src/features/commissions.js` + `commission-gen.js`.
- **Logbook (`src/features/logbook.js`):** each submarine keeps its own log (distance, dives, time submerged, deepest dive, dives below 30 / 60 / 90 m, snapshots) and slowly climbs levels 1–10 (many hours to the top). Levels grant only tiny handling refinements (`sub.tweaks`: turning, thrust efficiency, softer hull bumps, quicker self-repair; each stays under +15% in total) and unlock some cosmetics. A quiet note appears at a level-up. Stored in `localStorage['drift-logbook']`.
- **Gallery (`src/features/gallery.js`):** a pin button in the album viewer copies up to 6 snapshots (thumbnail + full image) into their own IndexedDB store `drift-gallery`, so they survive selling or deleting the original. Visitors leave a small entry fee every 5 minutes of play (a toast like "Gallery visitors left ◎120"); the amount depends on a hidden quality of the pinned shots and never shows per shot. Modest: typically ~500 pearls/hour, hard ceiling under ~2k/hour.
- **Wardrobe (`src/features/wardrobe.js`, data in `src/features/cosmetics.js`):** paint schemes (5 per sub), headlight beam colours and propeller blades, purely cosmetic and owned per submarine. A few unlock with logbook levels, most are bought with pearls (◎500–8,000, scaled by sub tier). The panel is a side sheet over the live game so the sub is the preview. Model API: `model.setPaint({ base, trim, accent, lamp, prop })` in `src/subs.js`.
- **Snapshots:** `P` or the camera button captures what the sub sees (hull hidden, up to 1920 px, JPEG). The album keeps the newest 100 in IndexedDB on your device; open it from "Album", view, download or delete. Nothing is uploaded.
- **Depth:** left-side gauge shows depth on a scale from 0 to the active sub's maximum dive depth (60 m for the Lemon without upgrades; it is rebuilt when you buy a hull strengthener or swap subs). The sub turns the gauge red and is pushed back near the limit even though the seabed goes deeper.
- **Touch:** left lever = thrust only (a 2-D joystick with the manoeuvring kit), drag elsewhere to steer, ▲ ▼ rise/dive. The "Controls" button (top right) cycles auto / touch / keys.
- **Debug:** `window.__sim` exposes `fleet`, `wallet` (`wallet.add(n)` grants pearls), `shop`, `swapSub(id)`, `stepZoom`, `lastShot` and the sim internals.
- World = pure function of `?seed=N`; only a 5×5 window of seabed chunks is ever in memory.
- **Places (landmarks):** `src/biomes.js` puts deterministic landmarks on a 256 m cell grid, a pure function of (seed, active submarine kind, x, z): big coral reefs (150–400 m across: spur-and-groove ridges, lagoon or maze variants, bommies, coral towers, table-coral plates, soft corals, down to ~9 m below the surface), hydrothermal vents (black smokers with GPU smoke, tube worms, bacterial mats; shallow 30–58 m fields and deep ones), magma rifts (glowing lava fissures that stay visible at night) and seamount volcanoes (glowing caldera lake, lava rivulets, ash, sparks and bubble streams), caves, natural arches, rock gardens, canyons, wrecks, trenches, drop-offs and seamount ridges. The active sub tilts the odds and the terrain character but never zeroes a kind: Lemon = balanced with gentle reefs; Manta (45 m) = more caves, arches, canyons, rock gardens, wrecks and reef mazes; Barracuda (45 m) = open water, long gentle shelves, big drop-offs and seamount ridges, fewer obstacles; Squid (95 m) = deeper terrain (trenches to 150 m+), many vent fields, magma rifts and volcanoes. Switching sub (`swap` event, `src/features/places.js`) dips to a short fog veil, rebuilds the chunks for the new profile and lifts the sub clear of the seabed. Live lava gives a gentle, forgiving hull-heat tick (red edge glow, scaled by the sub's fragility). Solid landmarks have sphere colliders like rocks. Per-chunk decor lives in `src/landmarks.js`, geometry in `src/models/landmarks*.js`, smoke/sparks in `src/plumes.js`.
- **Places API / debug:** `field.biomeAt(x, z)` → weights `{ reef, bigreef, open, deep, vent, magma, cave, trench, wreck }`; `field.featuresNear(x, z, radius)` → `[{ type, id, x, y, z, r }]` (types: bigreef, vent, magma, volcano, cave, arch, wreck, garden, canyon, trench, dropoff, seamount); `field.findFeature(type | [types], fromX, fromZ, maxR)` nearest feature; `field.profile` / `field.setProfile(kind)` (call `terrain.reset()` afterwards). In the console: `__sim.goto('volcano')` jumps near the nearest one (opts `{ dist, up, yaw, pitch }`), `__sim.gotoXZ(x, z, y?)`; `__sim.terrain.worstMs` is the slowest chunk build so far.
