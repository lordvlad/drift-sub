# Drift

A quiet underwater exploration toy: steer a small yellow submarine through an endless, procedurally generated sea (three.js).

- **Desktop:** `W`/`S` thrust, `A`/`D` turn, mouse to steer (click to capture), `Space` rise, `C` dive, `Shift` burst; with the manoeuvring kit `Q`/`E` strafe left/right.
- **Waves:** sea state follows your local wind (Open-Meteo, location rounded to ~11 km, never stored); falls back to a slow random drift if location is denied or offline. The "Waves" button cycles auto → still → calm → windy → gusty → stormy.
- **Controls UI:** every control is an icon button (hover for the label); tapping one briefly shows its new state (e.g. "lamps on") in the same quiet line as the calming notes.
- **Light:** time of day follows your clock (dawn, morning, day, evening, night hues; "Time" button overrides). The sub starts **without** lamps: buy the headlights in the shop (per sub), then `F` or the "Lamps" button cycles auto (on when dark or deep) / on / off. Until then the lamp buttons and `F` are hidden / inactive.
- **Fullscreen:** a dismissible banner suggests it a few seconds after diving (hidden where the Fullscreen API is missing, e.g. iPhone Safari).
- **PWA:** installable (manifest + service worker; works offline after the first visit). Add `?nosw` to skip the service worker.
- **Viewfinder:** the camera button (or `P`) enters viewfinder mode: first-person view from the sub's nose, you can turn/aim (mouse, `A`/`D`, drag) but not accelerate or strafe. `Space`/`Enter`/shutter takes the shot, the Lamps button (or `F`, if you own headlights) works in it, `Esc`/Exit leaves. With a zoom objective the magnifier button, mouse wheel or `+`/`−` step through 1× / 2× / 4× (FOV 58° narrows to 31° / 16°; the snapshot and its scoring use the zoomed FOV, so a distant animal in a 4× shot counts like a close one; zoom resets when you leave).
- **Shop:** the cart button opens a full-screen shop with your pearl balance (◎), upgrades for the active sub and the four submarines. Buying shows a toast; buttons are disabled when you can't afford the item or lack its prerequisite. Upgrades are bought **per submarine** and never transfer. Items: headlights ◎400; zoom objective 2× ◎1,200; zoom objective 4× ◎4,000 (needs 2×); hull repair kit ◎150 (consumable, repairs the active sub fully at once; disabled while undamaged); propeller Mk II ◎2,000 (+20% thrust), Mk III ◎6,000 (+40% total, needs Mk II); hull strengthener I ◎1,500 (+10 m max dive depth), II ◎5,000 (+20 m total, needs I); manoeuvring kit ◎3,500 (sideways thrusters with the same acceleration and inertia as the main motor; on touch the left lever becomes a 2-D joystick: x = strafe, y = thrust, never turning). Snapshot prices stay hidden until sold; there is no sell-all.
- **Submarines:** Lemon (free, 60 m, the all-rounder), Manta (◎45,000, 45 m, damage ×1.5, thrust ×1.05, turning ×1.6: very agile), Barracuda (◎80,000, 45 m, damage ×1.7, thrust ×1.7: fast), Squid (◎150,000, 95 m: deepest, damage ×2.4, thrust ×0.9: most fragile). Each sub keeps its own upgrades and its own hull damage. Once you own a second sub a swap button appears in the top bar: it cycles to the next owned sub (or use "Switch" in the shop), swapping the model in place and keeping position, heading and speed. You can't switch while deeper than the other sub could survive. Fleet state (`drift-fleet`) and pearls persist on this device; `src/fleet.js` holds the stats and item catalogue.
- **Selling:** every snapshot is priced when taken (pearls ◎): animals seen (rarer = more, bigger in frame and nearer the centre = more, more individuals = more), species variety, coral and seaweed in the background, visible light rays (none at night), golden hour and deep-dive bonuses. Prices are hidden: you only learn what a shot was worth after selling it, so choose what to sell by judging the composition yourself. Compositions you already sold are remembered (up to 400, on this device): each earlier sale of a similar shot (same species, group size, side of frame, scenery, depth band and light) cuts the price by ×0.55, down to ×0.1. Pearls are kept on this device.
- **Shy animals:** how far animals keep from the sub grows steeply with its speed. Drift in slowly for close-ups; rush in and fish schools scatter (individual fish dart away), sharks drop their curiosity and bolt, dolphins/orcas/mantas/giant squid turn and speed off, whales can't outrun you but lumber away. Swim speed is flexible: each animal cruises, sprints when scared (sharks ~11 m/s, dolphins ~11, orcas ~12, giant squid jets ~8, mantas ~6, whales only ~1.5× cruise) with real acceleration, and tires after a long sprint.
- **Time of day:** at night about half the fish schools are hidden in the reef, firefly squid are much more common, sharks and giant squid show up more, dolphins and mantas less; dusk and dawn sit in between.
- **Species & depths (m):** fish — lyretail anthias 2–28, yellow tang 2–30, blue chromis 3–40, blue-green chromis 3–32, moon wrasse 4–38, emperor red snapper 15–55, royal gramma 8–48 (rare), sardine 3–50, golden trevally 6–45, blue mackerel 8–60, yellowtail fusilier 5–40, glassy sweeper 28–60 (rare), rainbow runner 5–55 (rare); sharks — blacktip 3–35, grey reef 5–45, tiger 12–60 (each with its own markings); whales — humpback 8–50, blue 15–60; squid — bigfin reef squid 2–30 (schools of 5–18), firefly squid 25–60 (glowing schools of 30–90, rare), giant squid 40–60 (solitary, very rare); manta ray 3–40; dolphins 1–25 (pods of 3–9); orcas 2–40 (pods of 2–6). Every large animal stays clear of the seabed. Definitions live in `src/species.js`.
- **Damage:** hidden, no health bar. Hard bumps hurt: rocks and steep ground most, coral and whales a lot, flat sand only a little; ploughing through seaweed scrapes the hull. The sub's fragility multiplies the damage. Damage shows as a red glow growing from the screen edges, a lower safe dive depth (down to 40% of the sub's maximum at worst), and cracks on the camera lens (visible in the viewfinder and baked into snapshots, which sell for much less). The sub repairs itself slowly (a full wreck mends in about 12 minutes of play); a repair kit fixes it at once.
- **Bestiary:** the book button opens 24 numbered pages, one per species. A page unlocks once you take a snapshot in which the animal is recognisable (at least ~3% of the frame); until then it shows only its number and a depth hint. Unlocked pages show your own snapshot (cropped around the animal; a later, closer shot replaces it), a photo from Wikipedia/Wikimedia Commons (loaded on demand), a short description, a fun fact and links to Wikipedia, iNaturalist and (for fish) FishBase. Progress is kept on this device.
- **Snapshots:** `P` or the camera button captures what the sub sees (hull hidden, up to 1920 px, JPEG). The album keeps the newest 100 in IndexedDB on your device; open it from "Album", view, download or delete. Nothing is uploaded.
- **Depth:** left-side gauge shows depth on a scale from 0 to the active sub's maximum dive depth (60 m for the Lemon without upgrades; it is rebuilt when you buy a hull strengthener or swap subs). The sub turns the gauge red and is pushed back near the limit even though the seabed goes deeper.
- **Touch:** left lever = thrust only (a 2-D joystick with the manoeuvring kit), drag elsewhere to steer, ▲ ▼ rise/dive. The "Controls" button (top right) cycles auto / touch / keys.
- **Debug:** `window.__sim` exposes `fleet`, `wallet` (`wallet.add(n)` grants pearls), `shop`, `swapSub(id)`, `stepZoom`, `lastShot` and the sim internals.
- World = pure function of `?seed=N`; only a 5×5 window of seabed chunks is ever in memory.

```
bun install
bun run dev      # build + serve on :8080
```

Pushing to `main` deploys `dist/` to GitHub Pages via `.github/workflows/deploy.yml`
